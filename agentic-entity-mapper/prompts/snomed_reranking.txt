=== CLINICAL EQUIVALENCE PRINCIPLE ===

A candidate is a valid match if it refers to the SAME clinical concept as the entity,
even if the wording is different. Focus on MEANING, not exact wording.

VALID matches include synonyms, paraphrases, singular/plural variants, colloquial-to-clinical terminology, and candidates that preserve clinically important qualifiers.

INVALID matches include candidates with a different scope, candidates that describe only a property or measurement of the entity, candidates that lose important qualifiers such as site or laterality, and genuinely different clinical concepts.

Treat the identity of the underlying referent separately from assertion context. A candidate must preserve
the same underlying clinical or administrative thing, including its kind, modality, and target or body site.
An additional purpose or qualifier may be represented separately in grounded structured context when the
candidate remains the correct underlying concept. A context-bearing candidate is invalid if it preserves
absence, refusal, uncertainty, or another assertion but replaces the underlying referent with a broader,
narrower, or different action/fact. Conversely, the correct underlying concept may be selected as a final
fallback when the assertion is represented separately in structured output.

Administrative or FHIR-resource facts may be represented by generic SNOMED findings when the exact proper name is not itself a terminology concept. In that case, evaluate the meaning of the fact in context rather than requiring the candidate to contain the proprietary name.

=== DECISION RULES ===

- Colloquial term matching a formal medical term → SELECT
- The candidate preserves ALL clinically important qualifiers (body site, severity, laterality)
- The candidate preserves the underlying referent or action itself. Assertion context alone is not enough.
- If the entity includes context such as absence, refusal, allergy, family history, temporality, or uncertainty, prefer a candidate that explicitly preserves that context over a more generic positive/base concept.
- Resolve the entity's assertion from the full sentence meaning and syntax, not from a fixed token window or the nearest context word. Shared assertions apply to every coordinated entity they semantically govern, but stop where a boundary, contrast, or new assertion changes scope.
- If the entity is an administrative or resource-intent fact, the candidate may be a valid match when it represents the same patient-level administrative fact even though it omits a brand, organization, or plan name.

NO_MATCH when:
- The candidate is a PROPERTY/CHARACTERISTIC of the entity, not the entity itself
- Important meaning-changing qualifiers are lost. A candidate may omit a qualifier only when the mapping preserves it explicitly as structured context and the candidate remains the correct underlying ontology concept. This principle applies to any qualifier and is not limited to named examples.
- The candidate adds an unstated reaction, complication, or outcome. Intensity words alone do not justify selecting a reaction-specific concept; prefer the base allergy/finding concept unless the context explicitly states the reaction or outcome.
- The entity is a composite concept with multiple distinct parts.
- The candidate refers to a genuinely DIFFERENT clinical concept
- The entity is patient demographic/PII metadata, a de-identification artifact, a note-section label, or documentation boilerplate rather than a clinical/admin fact to represent as an ontology mapping. Examples include age-only mentions, names, contact details, addresses, identifiers, and masked de-identification tokens; these examples are not exhaustive. Do not reject clinically meaningful quantities or strengths when they belong to medications, tests, or procedures.

- Context is being assigned only because a trigger-like word occurs nearby, even though the sentence meaning does not place the entity inside that context's scope.
- The candidate is a broader negative category or symptom group rather than the specific finding/procedure/substance expressed by the entity.
- The candidate preserves an assertion such as refusal or absence but changes the underlying kind, modality,

- The candidate only mentions the same administrative domain but does not represent the same fact expressed in context.
